## Hall House: five-minute church interpretation

The user attached a hand-drawn set of curves for energy, melody, choir, and soprano. The written note “HIGH SOPRANO = LOW CHOIR” was interpreted as relative prominence, rather than an instruction about low choir pitch. The drawn curves have no exact time scale; the following timing is an arrangement interpretation.

| Target time | Arrangement |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:55 | Quiet solo; first melodic arch; faint choir where indicated. |
| 0:55–1:25 | Modest choir rise while the solo rests. |
| 1:25–2:30 | Retreat, then the solo's second melodic arch over very quiet choir. |
| 2:30–3:25 | Fall toward near silence. |
| 3:25–3:55 | Choir begins to emerge; soprano ends around 3:45. |
| 3:55–4:50 | Choir alone on sustained ah; gradual rise into the largest climax around 4:40–4:50. |
| 4:50–5:00 | All singers stop; only the natural reverberation remains. |

The acoustic direction asks for a large mixed choir in a vast stone church, beginning on a single consonant-free “ah”. Individual singers have independent natural vibrato, small pitch drift, and slightly different timing. The requested 6–10-second diffuse reverb overlaps successive cycles and reflections. Slow beating and changing interference should create a shimmering, rippling, bubbling, organically pulsating texture. The choir spreads gradually from unison into close luminous harmony, with extremely slow neighboring pitch movements. This is a desired acoustic result, not a claim that Suno implements a physical simulation of hundreds of singers.
